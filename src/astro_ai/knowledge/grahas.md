# The 9 Grahas — Consolidated Reference

The nine grahas (planets) of Vedic astrology, with their classical
attributes, mutual relationships, and interpretive rules. This is the
reference that per-planet articles and LLM prompts should draw on for
foundational rules — separate from the individual planet articles
which give narrative depth.

**Primary sources referenced:**
- P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, *Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*,
  Ch. 3 "Planets" (§3.1–3.3) (henceforth **Rao**)
- BV Raman, *Astrology for Beginners*, Ch. II §pp. 5-13 (henceforth
  **Raman**)
- Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra (**BPHS**) — for classical dignity
  rules and karakas
- Phaladeepika (**Phala**) — cross-cited where relevant
- Cross-links: rashis in `rashis.md`; houses in `houses.md`; aspects
  in `aspects.md`; karakas summary in `vedic_reference.md §5A`.

**Scope:** the 7 "real" grahas (Sun through Saturn) + the 2 chaayaa
grahas (shadow planets: Rahu and Ketu). Each planet's individual
article (`/learn/{planet}-in-vedic-astrology/`) covers narrative
depth; this file covers **the rules that combine across planets**.

|---|----------|---------|--------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Surya | Sun | Fiery, kingly | Rama (came from Sun) |
| 2 | Chandra | Moon | Watery, kingly | Krishna (came from Moon) |
| 3 | Mangal | Mars | Fiery, martial | Narasimha (half-man, half-lion) |

Rahu and Ketu are **mathematical points** — they don't have physical
bodies. They mark where the Moon's orbit around Earth crosses the
plane of Earth's orbit around the Sun. But Vedic astrology treats
them as full grahas for interpretive purposes.

## 2. Benefics vs Malefics (Rao §3.2.2)

**Natural benefics** (saumya / subha grahas):
- **Jupiter (Guru)** — the pure benefic
- **Venus (Shukra)** — the pure benefic
- **Waxing Moon** (Shukla Paksha, first 15 days after new moon)
- **Mercury** — benefic when alone or with more natural benefics

**Natural malefics** (kroora / paapa grahas):
- **Sun (Surya)** — mild malefic (kingly authority carries pressure)
- **Mars (Mangal)** — strong malefic
- **Rahu** — strong malefic
- **Ketu** — strong malefic
- **Waning Moon** (Krishna Paksha, first 15 days after full moon)
- **Mercury** — malefic when joined by more natural malefics

**Interpretive rule:** benefics give favourable results; malefics
give unfavourable results. But this is modified heavily by house
lordship (see `houses.md §Classification`) — a natural malefic ruling
a good house can produce great results, and a natural benefic ruling
a difficult house (Kendradhipati Dosha) can create trouble.

## 3. Main governance (karakas) (Rao §3.2.3)

Each planet is the **karaka** (natural significator) of a specific
life domain. This is the most-used relationship in Vedic astrology.

|--------|---------|

| Moon | Mind (manas), mother, emotions, home, water |

| Saturn | Grief, longevity, service, discipline, delays |

## 4. Planetary Cabinet (Rao §3.2.5)

Rao's teaching organises the grahas as a royal court:

- **Sun & Moon** — Kings (raja). Together they rule the "court".
- **Mars** — Army chief (senapati). Commands force.
- **Mercury** — Prince (kumara). Brilliant, princely, learning.
- **Jupiter & Venus** — Ministers (mantri). Advisors to the kings.
- **Saturn** — Servant (bhritya). Executes the mundane work.
- **Rahu & Ketu** — Army (sena). Collective, transformative force.

This isn't just poetic — it feeds into interpretation. When a
"servant planet" (Saturn) sits with a "king" (Sun), the classical
reading is one of tension (servant doesn't easily coexist with the
king's dignity). When a minister (Jupiter) sits with a king (Sun),
the reading is one of counsel and wisdom-guided leadership.

|--------|-----|---------|-------|------|--------------------|-----------  |
| Sun | Male | Fire | Kshatriya | Sattwik | Bones | Ayana (6 months) |
| Moon | Female | Water | Vaisya | Sattwik | Blood | Muhurta (48 min) |
| Mars | Male | Fire | Kshatriya | Taamasik | Marrow | Week |
| Mercury | Neutral (female per Rao) | Earth | Vaisya | Raajasik | Skin | Ritu (2 months) |
| Jupiter | Male | Ether | Brahmana | Sattwik | Fat | Month |
| Venus | Female | Water | Brahmana | Raajasik | Semen (reproductive) | Fortnight |

**Note on Rao's guna classification (Rao §3.2.10):** Rao specifically
warns against a common misconception — sattwa doesn't mean "not
hurting others". Sattwa means "the state of being true". Someone who
punishes a wrongdoer without any passion or ego can still be
saattwik. Sun is saattwik despite being a warrior; Lord Rama was
saattwik despite killing Ravana. Sattwa = pure truthfulness; Rajas =
passionate energy; Tamas = dark/depraved.

**Dhatu rulership use:** if a planet is afflicted in the chart, the
body tissue it rules may show weakness. Weak Moon → blood problems.
Weak Sun → bone/skeletal issues. Weak Jupiter → fat/lipid issues.

## 8. Planetary Dignities (Rao §3.3) — the master table

The most important interpretive table in all of Vedic astrology.
A planet's strength depends on which sign it's in. Below: own sign
(swakshetra), exaltation (uchcha), debilitation (neecha), moolatrikona.

| Planet | Own rashi(s) | Exalted in (deep point) | Debilitated in (deep point) | Moolatrikona |
|--------|--------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------|
| Sun | Leo | Aries (10°) | Libra (10°) | Leo |
| Moon | Cancer | Taurus (3°) | Scorpio (3°) | Taurus |
| Mars | Aries & Scorpio | Capricorn (28°) | Cancer (28°) | Aries |
| Mercury | Gemini & Virgo | Virgo (15°) | Pisces (15°) | Virgo |
| Jupiter | Sagittarius & Pisces | Cancer (5°) | Capricorn (5°) | Sagittarius |
| Venus | Taurus & Libra | Pisces (27°) | Virgo (27°) | Libra |
| Saturn | Capricorn & Aquarius | Libra (20°) | Aries (20°) | Aquarius |
| Rahu | Aquarius | Gemini (some say Taurus) | Sagittarius (some say Scorpio) | Virgo |
| Ketu | Scorpio | Sagittarius (some say Scorpio) | Gemini (some say Taurus) | Pisces |

### 8.1 Rao's analogy for the four dignities

- **Own rashi** — like one's home. Comfortable, natural, functional.
- **Moolatrikona** — like one's office. Powerful, duty-minded,
  formally executing one's role.
- **Exaltation rashi** — like one's favorite party or picnic. Excited,
  overflowing energy, peak positive expression.
- **Debilitation rashi** — like being stuck at one's worst party.
  Unhappy, out of place, results are constrained or twisted.

### 8.2 Sub-divisions within own+moolatrikona signs

For planets whose moolatrikona is in the same sign as own rashi
(Sun, Jupiter, Saturn), Rao specifies degree-based sub-rules:

- **Sun in Leo**: 0°–20° gives moolatrikona results; 20°–30° gives
  own-rashi results.
- **Mars in Aries**: 0°–12° gives moolatrikona results; 12°–30° gives
  own-rashi results.
- **Jupiter in Sagittarius**: 0°–10° moolatrikona; 10°–30° own-rashi.
- **Saturn in Aquarius**: 0°–20° moolatrikona; 20°–30° own-rashi.
- **Venus in Libra**: first half moolatrikona; second half own-rashi.
- **Moon in Taurus**: 0°–3° exaltation; 3°–30° moolatrikona.
- **Mercury in Virgo**: 0°–15° exaltation; 15°–20° moolatrikona;
  20°–30° own-rashi.

Practically: the earlier the degree within these signs, the "higher"
the dignity expression.

## 9. Directional Strength (Digbala) (Rao §3.2.15)

Each planet gets strength when placed in its "favoured" house
(direction from the ascendant).

| Planet | Digbala house | Direction |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| Jupiter, Mercury | 1st house (Lagna) | East |
| Sun, Mars | 10th house (meridian) | South |
| Moon, Venus | 4th house (nadir) | North |
| Saturn | 7th house | West |

Rahu and Ketu have no traditional Digbala assignment.

**Interpretive use:** a planet in its Digbala house is significantly
strengthened even if it's not in its own sign or exalted. Sun in the
10th house (Digbala + karaka of career) is a very strong classical
combination for career prominence. Similarly Saturn in the 7th
(Digbala + karaka of partnership) can give durable if late-blooming
marriages.

---

## 10. Time-based strength (Rao §3.2.15)

Beyond dignity + direction, planets have additional strength based on
time:

- **Night-strong planets:** Moon, Mars, Saturn — perform strongest
  during the night half of the day.
- **Day-strong planets:** Sun, Jupiter, Venus — perform strongest
  during the daytime.
- **Always strong:** Mercury.

- **Krishna paksha (dark lunar fortnight) strong:** natural malefics.
- **Shukla paksha (bright lunar fortnight) strong:** natural benefics.

- **Dakshina ayana (Sun in Cn–Sg, roughly July–December) strong:**
  natural malefics.
- **Uttara ayana (Sun in Cp–Ge, roughly January–June) strong:**
  natural benefics.

These are components of Kalabala (temporal strength) in the full
Shadbala (six-fold strength) calculation.

## 11. Planetary friendships (naisargika + tatkalika)

Every planet has natural friends, enemies, and neutrals. This affects
how the planet behaves when placed in another planet's sign or
conjunct with it.

### 11.1 Natural (Naisargika) friendships

| Planet | Friends | Enemies | Neutrals |
|--------|---------|---------|----------|
| Sun | Moon, Mars, Jupiter | Venus, Saturn | Mercury |
| Moon | Sun, Mercury | none | Mars, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn |
| Mars | Sun, Moon, Jupiter | Mercury | Venus, Saturn |
| Mercury | Sun, Venus | Moon | Mars, Jupiter, Saturn |
| Jupiter | Sun, Moon, Mars | Mercury, Venus | Saturn |
| Venus | Mercury, Saturn | Sun, Moon | Mars, Jupiter |
| Saturn | Mercury, Venus | Sun, Moon, Mars | Jupiter |

**Rahu/Ketu:** classically friendly with Venus and Saturn; enemies of
Sun, Moon, Mars; neutral with others. Traditions vary here more than
for the classical 7.

### 11.2 Temporary (Tatkalika) friendships

Planets in the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 10th, 11th, or 12th house **from each
other** become temporary friends. Planets in the 1st, 5th, 6th, 7th,
8th, 9th from each other become temporary enemies.

### 11.3 Combined (Panchadha)

Combine natural + temporary via the standard grid:

| Nat friend + Tat friend | = Adhi mitra (great friend) |
| Nat friend + Tat enemy | = Sama (neutral) |
| Nat neutral + Tat friend | = Mitra (friend) |
| Nat neutral + Tat enemy | = Satru (enemy) |
| Nat enemy + Tat friend | = Sama (neutral) |
| Nat enemy + Tat enemy | = Adhi satru (great enemy) |

Used in advanced Sthana Bala (positional strength) calculations.

### 12.2 What the four books actually say

Every line number below was taken with `grep -n` on `pdftotext -layout` output
(or, for Raman, on the plain-text file directly) and read back with
`sed -n 'Np'`. Printed pages were resolved by **single-page re-extraction**
(`pdftotext -f N -l N`) — see §12.8, and §12.9 for why the first draft's page
numbers were all wrong by one.

`book1-for-CD.pdf` is a **rotated two-page spread**: each PDF page carries two
printed pages side by side, and `-layout` interleaves their lines. Printed
pages for that book are therefore cited as the **spread** (e.g. "pp. 91–92"),
because which column a line belongs to is not reliably recoverable from the
`-layout` geometry — the column offsets differ page to page. The one exception
is Lesson #30, where the two halves carry different lesson headers and the
attribution is unambiguous.

**Rao, *Lessons* Vol. I, Lesson #2** (printed pp. **5–6**, PDF page 11; lines

> "when Ve and Su are close, Ve loses its energy to Su. This is called
> combustion. Ve gets combusted. This is called 'Maudhyam' Its called in
> Sanskrit as 'Asta Gandha'. Any planet close to Su gets combust (burned). Ju
> combustion is not bad. The most combustion to any planet happens to Ve."

and the exception (lines **334–340**):

> "There is an exception to combustion. All planets close to Su get combust and

### 12.3 Budha-Aditya and the 6° orb — the orb is UNSOURCED (#218)

**The rule site:** `chart.py`, in `_sun_yogas()` — the Budha-Aditya branch:

```python
if abs(mercury.longitude - sun.longitude) < 6.0:
    combust_note = " (weakened — Mercury is combust within 6° of Sun)"
```

**What IS now sourced.** Rao, *Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*,
**§11.2.4 "Budha-Aaditya Yoga (Nipuna Yoga)"** (heading at line **4229**,
printed p. **113**, PDF page 125; body lines **4231–4245**, printed p. **114**,
PDF page 126):

> "Note: If Mercury is too close to Sun, he is combust (asta or astangata).
> Yogas formed by combust planets lose some of their power to do good. This
> yoga is the most powerful in divisional charts like D-10. In rasi chart also,
> it can give results if Mercury is not combust." (lines 4239–4242)

So three things are sourced: the yoga's trigger (Sun and Mercury in one sign,
lines **4233–4234**), the name *Nipuna* — *"Nipuna means an expert"* (line
**4237**), confirming the label `chart.py` renders — and **the principle that
combustion weakens this yoga**.

**What is NOT sourced: the number 6.** Rao writes *"too close"* and gives no
degree. No source in the corpus states a Budha-Aditya orb. The 6° is the
repo's own invention.

**And 6° is inconsistent with the repo's own definition of combust.** Rao's
condition is literally the word *combust*, and §12.1 — now verified against
Raman p. 197 — defines Mercury combust as **14°** direct / **13°** retrograde.
`chart.py` therefore applies a third number to a rule whose only source defers

### 12.5 Muhurat combustion — degree-orb vs days-from-heliacal (#219)

**The rule site:** `muhurat.py`'s `_combustion_flags()` computes combustion by

### 12.8 Citation hygiene for this section

**Printed page numbers are in the running HEADER — the FIRST line of each
`-layout` page — in all four books.** Resolve one by re-extracting the single
page (`pdftotext -layout -f N -l N file.pdf -`) and reading its first line. Do
**not** infer it from a contiguous `sed -n 'A,Bp'` range: the number that
appears *after* a block of text is the header of the *next* page, and reading it
as a footer puts every citation one page late. That is exactly how the first
draft of this section got all ten of its printed pages wrong (§12.9).

printed pages side by side and `-layout` interleaves their lines, so (a) a
contiguous line range spanning a quoted passage will contain unrelated text
from the facing column — quote specific lines, never ranges — and (b) the
column-to-printed-page mapping is not reliably recoverable, because the column
offsets vary from page to page. Cite the spread.

## 13. Retrogression (Vakri)

Any planet other than Sun and Moon can be retrograde (appearing to
move backward from Earth's perspective — an optical illusion caused
by Earth "passing" the outer planets in its orbit).

**Classical rule:** retrograde planets are *stronger* in Vedic
astrology (unlike Western tradition which often reads retrogrades as
weakened). A retrograde planet's karakas play out with unusual
intensity — sometimes as compulsive themes, sometimes as
extraordinary gifts.

- **Retrograde benefic** → unusual excess of that planet's positive
  themes (e.g., retrograde Jupiter → deep learning, expansion of
  meaning).
- **Retrograde malefic** → unusual excess of that planet's difficult
  themes (e.g., retrograde Mars → volcanic bursts of will;
  retrograde Saturn → deep delays with eventual mastery).

Rahu and Ketu are always retrograde by convention (they move
backward through the zodiac).

## 14. How to phrase graha rules in a reading

- **Don't lead with technical dignity terms.** Not "your Sun is
  debilitated in Libra" — say "your sense of self operates in a
  domain where its natural authority is diluted" (concrete meaning).
- **Frame karakas as themes, not fates.** "Your Venus governs marriage
  and it's afflicted" ≠ "you will not marry". It might mean
  "relationships require conscious work in ways they don't for
  everyone".
- **When a planet has multiple dignity signals, sum them honestly.**
  A Sun in Leo (own sign) but combust by proximity to the Sun (wait
  — Sun can't be combust by itself; example) — pick a real one: Mars
  in Cancer (debilitated) in the 10th house (Digbala) is a
  contradiction the classical rules explicitly recognise. Say so.
- **Layer natural + temporary friendship carefully.** Two planets in
  a mutual friendship-house-relationship, both natural friends,
  behave differently from two natural enemies in an aversive
  positional relationship.
- **Never use "combust" alone as a diagnosis.** Combust Mercury with
  strong Jupiter aspect can still function well. Combustion is
  weakening, not disabling.
