# Divisional Charts (Vargas) — Reading Sub-Charts for Life Areas

**Divisional charts** (called **Vargas** or **Amsas**) are sub-charts
derived from the main Rasi Chakra by subdividing each sign into
smaller parts. Each varga specialises in a specific life area — the
main chart gives the overall picture, but the varga gives the fine-
grained truth about that area. Classical Vedic astrology uses up to
16 different vargas (**Shodashavargas**), though most practical
readings use 3-5.

**Primary sources referenced:**
- BV Raman, *Astrology for Beginners*, Ch. II, pp. 23-30 (henceforth
  **Raman**) — covers Navamsha construction in detail
- Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra (**BPHS**), Ch. 6-8 — the classical
  source for all 16 vargas
- Cross-links: houses in `houses.md`; nakshatras in
  `vedic_reference.md §27`.

**Scope of this file:** the vargas actually used in modern practice.
The obscure ones (D-16, D-20, D-24, D-27, D-40, D-45, D-60) get
mentioned but not detailed — they're used only in specialist readings.

---

## Why sub-charts exist

Consider two people born in the same city on the same day, minutes
apart. Their Rasi charts are almost identical — same Ascendant sign,
same planetary positions. But their careers, marriages, and children
turn out entirely differently.

Divisional charts explain why. **The Rasi divides each 30° sign into
segments that are pinned to the exact minute of birth.** In the D-9
(Navamsha), each sign is divided into 9 parts of 3°20' each — so a
planet at 5° of Aries and a planet at 25° of Aries land in different
Navamsha signs. The finer the division, the more sensitive to birth-
minute precision.

**Practical implication:** if the birth time is off by more than 4
minutes, the D-9 Ascendant can shift signs. This is why classical
astrologers ask for exact birth times, and why "birth time
rectification" is a whole sub-discipline.

## The 16 vargas at a glance

Only D-1, D-9, and D-10 are used in most everyday readings. D-7,
D-12, and D-30 come in for specific life-area questions.

|-------|-----------|-----------|--------------------|

| **D-3 Drekkana** | 3 | 10° | Siblings, courage, physical vitality |

| **D-16 Shodashamsa** | 16 | 1°52'30" | Vehicles, comforts |
| **D-20 Vimshamsa** | 20 | 1°30' | Spiritual practice, worship |
| **D-24 Chaturvimshamsa** | 24 | 1°15' | Learning, education (post-primary) |
| **D-27 Bhamsa / Saptavimshamsa** | 27 | 1°06'40" | Strengths and weaknesses (nakshatra-based) |
| **D-30 Trimshamsa** | 30 | 1° | Misfortunes, doshas, character weaknesses |

**Practical usage note:** everyday consultation uses D-1 + D-9 + D-10.

readings emphasise D-10. Deep karmic / spiritual readings use D-30,
D-40, D-45, D-60 — but these are only meaningful with birth times
accurate to the second, which most seekers don't have.

## D-1 — Rashi Chakra (the main chart)

The zodiacal chart everyone means by "your Kundli". Each planet's
sign, house, and nakshatra come from the D-1. This is the base — all
other vargas are constructed from it.

**Use D-1 for:** overall life picture, personality (via Ascendant),
general planetary strengths, dasha/gochar readings, most predictions.

For the twelve houses and their significations, see `houses.md`.

## D-9 — Navamsha (the second-most-important chart)

**The Navamsha is unique in Vedic astrology.** [Raman, Ch. II, p. 23]
No other tradition puts this much weight on a single divisional chart.
Classical astrologers often say: **"A planet's real strength is judged
by its Navamsha, not its Rasi."**

### What Navamsha rules

- **Marriage and spouse** — the classical primary use. The D-9 lagna
  and its lord describe the spouse. Planets in the 7th of D-9 describe
  marriage quality more accurately than planets in the 7th of D-1.
- **Dharma and hidden strength** — the D-9 reveals a planet's *inner*
  quality regardless of its outer Rasi placement.
- **Second half of life** — traditionally, planetary effects in the
  first half of life come from D-1; the second half from D-9.
- **Yoga confirmation** — a yoga that appears in the D-1 is
  considered activated only if it ALSO appears in the D-9. This is
  a critical filter for reading raja yogas.
  **[UNSOURCED, and CONTESTED by Rao — see "Yoga strength across the vargas"
  below. Kept because it may be real practice somewhere, but it has no
  citation in this corpus and Rao's rule is a ten-chart count, not a D-9
  gate. Do not render this to a reader as classical.]**

### How Navamsha is constructed [Raman, Ch. II, pp. 23-24]

Each 30° sign is divided into **9 parts of 3°20' each** = one
Navamsha. The 9 Navamshas in a sign are ruled by the lords of nine
consecutive signs, starting from a rule that depends on the sign's
quality:

| Rasi type | Navamsha count starts from |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Fire signs** (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius) | Aries |
| **Earth signs** (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn) | Capricorn |
| **Air signs** (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius) | Libra |
| **Water signs** (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces) | Cancer |

**Worked example** [adapted from Raman, p. 24]: A planet at Libra
20°10'.

- 20°10' ÷ 3°20' = falls in the **7th** Navamsha of Libra
- Libra is an air sign, so Navamsha counting starts from Libra itself
- Count 7 signs from Libra: Libra → Scorpio → Sagittarius → Capricorn
  → Aquarius → Pisces → **Aries**
- The planet's Navamsha sign is Aries; its Navamsha lord is Mars.

**Modern shortcut:** any chart-computing engine (like Shubh Bhagya's)
computes the D-9 automatically from birth details. The mechanical
construction matters only for astrologers who want to verify by hand.

### Key Navamsha rules to memorise

- **Vargottama planet** — a planet in the same sign in both D-1 and
  D-9 (e.g., Sun in Leo in both) is called *Vargottama* and gains
  significant strength regardless of other factors. Vargottama Sun,
  Moon, or Ascendant lord is a classical strength booster.
- **Debilitation cancellation via D-9** — a planet debilitated in
  D-1 but exalted in D-9 (or vice versa) gets **Neechabhanga**
  (debility cancellation). The debilitation is functionally removed.
- **D-9 Ascendant** — describes the spouse's appearance and character
  more accurately than D-1 for marriage purposes. When astrologers
  say "his wife is Taurus-type" they usually mean D-9 Lagna is Taurus.
- **Planets from D-9 Lagna** — the 7th house of D-9 is the primary
  marriage indicator, more so than the 7th of D-1.

### How to phrase Navamsha in a reading

- **Don't lead with "the D-9"** — say "your marriage chart" or
  "your inner strength chart". Technical labels lose readers.
- **When the D-1 gives a mixed reading and the D-9 gives a clean one,
  trust the D-9 for the deeper truth.** This is what "the Navamsha
  is more important" means in practice.
- **Never predict marriage timing from D-9 alone.** Timing comes from
  Dasha (see `vedic_reference.md §5`) + Gochar (transits). The D-9
  describes *what* the marriage will be like; not *when*.

## D-10 — Dashamsa (career chart)

Each sign divided into 10 parts of 3° each. The Dashamsa is to career
what Navamsha is to marriage — it reveals the *real* nature of one's
work life, especially the mid-to-late career phase.

**Use Dashamsa for:**
- Actual profession (as distinct from what the 10th of D-1 promises)
- Career shifts, especially the second career
- Public reputation and status
- Judging Raja Yogas — **one input, not the test.** D-10 is one of the ten
  Dasavarga charts Rao counts over, and §36.4 makes it the chart of
  "achievements in society"; §12.5's Vajpayee example explains a career from
  D-10's SAV alone. But Rao's magnitude rule is the ten-chart count, orb and
  freedom from affliction — see "Yoga strength across the vargas" below

**Common rules:**
- **10th lord of D-1 also strong in D-10** = career matches early
  ambition; the promised profession actually manifests.
- **10th lord weak in D-10** = late career shift, or the promise of
  the D-1 doesn't quite land.
- **Sun strong in D-10** = government service, senior authority
  role, family-business leadership.
- **Saturn strong in D-10** = long steady career in structured
  hierarchies; often service to the public.

## D-7 — Saptamsa (progeny chart)

Each sign divided into 7 parts of ~4°17' each. The Saptamsa is the
primary chart for children.

**Use Saptamsa for:**
- Number of children (approximate, via classical count rules)
- Nature and quality of the parent-child relationship
- Which children will be prosperous vs struggle
- Fertility issues (when combined with 5th house of D-1)

**Note:** D-7 rules from classical texts often assume many children
in a normal family — modern applicability requires softening.
Reading it as "the *quality* of the child-relationship" rather than
"how many children" is often more accurate.

---

## D-12 — Dwadashamsa (parents chart)

Each sign divided into 12 parts of 2°30' each. The Dwadashamsa gives
information about both parents and ancestral karma.

**Use Dwadashamsa for:**
- Parents' longevity, health, prosperity (compare with D-1's 9th and
  4th houses)
- Ancestral inheritance patterns
- Karmic themes carried into the family lineage

Less commonly read than D-9 / D-10 but essential when the seeker is
asking specifically about their parents or the family "story".

---

## D-3 — Drekkana (siblings and vitality chart)

Each sign divided into 3 parts of 10° each. Used for:
- Sibling relationships (older/younger via the 3rd and 11th of D-3)
- Physical vitality (the first Drekkana of the Ascendant sign is
  particularly important — it maps to the head/upper body)

Classical rule: for questions about the physical body's strength,
consult the first Drekkana of the Ascendant sign in the D-1.

---

## D-30 — Trimshamsa (misfortunes and character weaknesses)

Each sign divided into 5 unequal parts (each of a different length,
5°/5°/8°/7°/5°) ruled by the five malefics-and-neutrals: Mars,
Saturn, Jupiter, Mercury, Venus (in a specific order that alternates
by even/odd signs).

**Use Trimshamsa for:**
- What character or health weaknesses are hidden in the chart
- Reading the "dark side" — patterns of trouble the person tends
  to attract
- Classical dosha analysis when the surface D-1 looks fine but
  the person's life shows repeated struggle

Rarely used in beginner readings but essential when a seeker's
experience doesn't match a superficially strong Rasi chart.

## How to use the vargas together

The **big-three practical workflow** for a chart:

1. **Read D-1 for the overall picture** — personality (Lagna),
   general planetary positions, dasha state, current gochar.
2. **Confirm with D-9** — is the yoga you spotted in D-1 also present
   in D-9? Is the planet debilitated in D-1 rescued by D-9? What is
   the D-9 lagna and its lord?
3. **Zoom in with D-10 for career questions**, **D-7 for children**,
   **D-12 for parents**.

**Never contradict yourself.** If D-1 shows a strong 10th house but
D-10 shows a weak lagna and afflicted 10th, don't pretend both are
true — say "the D-1 promises career prominence, but the D-10 warns
that the delivery is uneven; expect a career that swings between
recognition and struggle."

**Vargottama check.** Before reading any planet's effect, check
whether it's Vargottama (same sign in D-1 and D-9). If yes, its
effects are amplified — for better AND worse.

## How to phrase vargas in a reading

- **Never mention "D-9" or "Navamsha" by name to a non-astrologer.**
  Say "your marriage chart shows..." — that's what it is functionally.
- **Frame vargas as zoom-lenses, not separate horoscopes.** A common
  reader misconception is that the D-9 is a "second chart" — it's not,
  it's the D-1 seen through a different filter.
- **Don't over-cite Vargottama** — mention it when it's decisive
  (Vargottama Lagna lord, Vargottama Moon), not for every planet.
- **When both D-1 and D-9 agree on a reading, say so.** "Both your
  main chart and your marriage chart show..." is more persuasive
  than either alone.
- **Never predict marriage/child dates from vargas alone.** Timing
  comes from Vimshottari Dasha + Gochar; vargas give *quality* not
  *when*.

### Yoga strength across the vargas — what Parashara actually requires

**This settles `prompts/natal.txt:65`. Verdict: CONTRADICTED.**

Rao's one Parashara-attributed rule about yogas and vargas is in **§11.7.2
"Magnitude of a Raaja Yoga"** (lines 6151–6234). Its three primary factors are
not varga-based at all:

1. the two planets must be **free from affliction by functional malefics**
   (line 6161);
2. the conjunction or aspect forming the yoga must be **close — "say, within 6°
   or so"** (lines 6163–6164), with a footnote: *"Anything greater than 5 or 6
   degrees is too large for a Raaja Yoga to give its full results."* (line
   6256). Rao's worked counter-example: Mercury at 2° and Venus at 26° of
   Taurus do form Dharma-Karmadhipati Yoga, but *"the two planets are too far
   apart for this yoga to give its full results"* (lines 6165–6171);
3. neither planet may be **combust, debilitated, in an inimical house, or in a
   bad avastha** (lines 6172–6173).

Then, and only then:

> **"In addition to the above factors, Sage Parasara recommended looking at the
> amsas occupied by the 2 planets as per Dasa Varga (ten division) scheme."**
> — lines 6174–6176

So the rule Rao attributes to Parashara is:

- a **count over the ten Dasavarga charts** (D-1, D-2, D-3, D-7, D-9, D-10,
  D-12, D-16, D-30, D-60), not a test on D-9;
- applied to **the two yoga-forming planets' own dignity** (own / exaltation /
  moolatrikona), not to whether the yoga re-forms in a sub-chart;
- **graded on a nine-step ladder**, not binary: count 0–1 is "ordinary", 2
  "a king who rules his people well", 3 "a good king with tremendous assets",
  4 "a great king respected by many kings", 5 "a great emperor who rules the
  whole world", and 6–9 *"only for divine persons"* (lines 6178–6198).

And Rao closes the section by denying exactly the word the prompt uses:

> **"Final Judgment: None of the above factors influences the end result
> completely. We should look at all the factors and make the final judgment."**
> — lines 6199–6200

His two worked examples make the point concretely. **Rajiv Gandhi** (lines
6203–6212) had Raja-Yoga planets in Simhaasanaamsa — count 5, the "emperor of
the whole world" rung — but they were more than 8° apart and afflicted, so
*"the Yoga is pretty weak … In the end, he only became India's prime minister
and not an 'emperor of the whole world'."* **Akbar** (lines 6213–6225) was a
great emperor with his Raja-Yoga planets only in Uttamaamsa and
Paarijaataamsa. The varga count neither guarantees nor withholds the result.

**Where D-9 genuinely does appear in Rao's Raja Yogas**, it appears as one
member of a set, never alone:

- *"If the same planet aspects lagna in the six divisional charts of shad
  vargas – Rasi, Navamsa, Hora, Drekkana, Dwadasamsa, Trimsamsa – then that
  planet gives a Raaja Yoga."* (§11.7.3 (7), lines 6295–6297) — six charts,
  and this **constitutes** a yoga rather than confirming one.
- *"If lagna in Rasi, Navamsa and Drekkana charts is occupied by a planet in
  own or exaltation sign, then the native becomes a king."* (§11.7.3 (9),
  lines 6306–6308) — three charts, and *"It can be different planets."*

**What Rao does say about yogas in divisional charts** is a re-interpretation
rule, not an amplification rule:

> **"Many yogas are applicable in divisional charts. When we use yogas in
> divisional charts, we should interpret the results ascribed to a yoga based
> on the matters signified by the divisional chart."**
> — §11.1, lines 5320–5323
>
> **"When using yogas in divisional charts, adapt the results to the matters
> signified by the chart. All the great results ascribed to some yogas may not
> materialize in reality, due to weaknesses in them. Unless a yoga is very
> strong, all the results cannot expected."**
> — §11.11 Conclusion, lines 6543–6547

And for the Ravi yogas specifically: *"they are less common in divisional
charts. One can apply these yogas in D-9 and D-10 in particular."* (§11.2,
lines 5351–5353) — **D-9 *and* D-10, as a pair, and as the charts in which to
*apply* a yoga, not as a confirmation gate.**

Finally, Rao names which chart "manifestation" belongs to, and it is not D-9:

> **"Rasi is the chart of physical manifestation; navamsa is the chart of
> dharma and inner self; and, dasamsa is the chart of achievements in
> society."**

### Ashtakavarga applies in every varga, not only D-1 [Rao §12.5, lines 7808–7881]

> *"There is a misconception that ashtakavarga is applicable only to rasi
> charts. Parasara does not say it … So all the analysis in the rest of his
> classic applies to all the divisional charts, unless Parasara explicitly
> mentions rasi chart."* — lines 7809–7813

Mechanically identical: the benefic houses for each planet with respect to the
eight references are the same in every varga, so BAV and SAV compute the same
way (lines 7831–7834). Rao's worked example is **Vajpayee** (lines 7836–7869):
rasi-chart SAV gives lagna 26 and the 10th 28 rekhas — *"just average"* — and
the career is explained only by D-10, whose lagna holds **35 rekhas**, the

explicit answer to "why was his career successful" is *"The answer lies in the
SAV of D-10."* (line 7864).

This is a second, independent Rao passage putting **career** manifestation in
**D-10** — and it is a worked chart, not a dictum.
