{# ─── MOBILE BOTTOM TAB BAR (#261, deferred out of #242) ────────────────── Five destinations, sticky, mobile only. `md:hidden` because the top nav already carries all of this from `md` up, and two navigations on one screen is worse than none. IT LIVES AT THE END OF , NOT IN _nav.html, AND THAT IS LOAD-BEARING. The first version sat at the bottom of _nav.html — which is included at the TOP of every page — so its five links came early in the DOM while painting at the bottom of the viewport. Tabbing out of them jumped backward into page content: one −Npx reversal per language on mobile, measured, which is the exact WCAG 2.4.3 violation #195 exists to prevent. Painted position and DOM position have to agree, so the bar is last in the document. That move cost it `active_page`, which is `{% set %}` inside each page's content block and therefore invisible out here. The active tab is derived from the request path instead — which is more robust anyway, since it cannot drift from a variable a page forgets to set. IT COSTS FOLD, AND THE FOLD IS GATED. A fixed bar does not move content, so scripts/measure_cta_fold.py reports no change — it measures DOCUMENT position. What the bar actually does is cover the bottom of the viewport. Measured: 75px tall, so the homepage CTA's real clearance at 375x667 goes from 129px to 86px. Re-measure with the browser, not with that harness, if the padding here ever changes. Z-ORDER. Two other fixed elements live at the bottom of the screen: the feedback FAB and #matching-sticky-cta. Both are lifted clear on mobile rather than left underneath — a submit button you cannot reach is a worse defect than the one this bar fixes. `aria-current="page"` rather than colour alone: the active tab is saffron, and colour must never be the only signal (WCAG 1.4.1). #} {% set _path = request.url.path %}
