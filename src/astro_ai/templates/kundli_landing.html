What is a Janma Kundli?

A Janma Kundli — literally "birth chart" — is a snapshot of the sky at the exact moment and location of your birth. Vedic astrology reads it not as fortune-telling but as a diagnostic: the arrangement of the nine grahas (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu, Ketu) across the twelve houses tells a classical astrologer where your natural strengths lie, what karmic patterns you'll navigate, and which life periods (dashas) will accelerate or slow certain areas of life.

The chart uses the sidereal Lahiri ayanamsa, the standard reference frame in Indian classical astrology. This is what makes Vedic astrology different from Western — the same birth date can put your Sun in a different sign because sidereal astrology accounts for the ~24° drift of the zodiac since the classical texts were written. Bhagya computes charts using Swiss Ephemeris, the same astronomical library serious astrologers use in their software.

Do you need your exact birth time?

Yes. The Lagna (Ascendant) changes sign every ~2 hours, and moves ~1° every 4 minutes. A wrong Lagna changes every house cusp — planets get reassigned to different houses, and the interpretation shifts completely. If you don't know your birth time:

  • • Check your birth certificate first — it's usually there.
  • • Ask your parents or a close family member.
  • • For a rough Moon-sign reading (Chandra Kundli) noon local time is a common convention, but the Ascendant will be unreliable.
  • • A classical astrologer can do a birth-time rectification by matching known life events to chart transits — but that's beyond a free-tool scope.

Sidereal vs Tropical — why Vedic looks different

Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac — signs anchored to the fixed background stars. Western astrology uses the tropical zodiac — signs anchored to the seasons (equinoxes and solstices).

Because of a slow wobble in Earth's axis (called precession), the two systems have drifted ~24° apart in the ~2,000 years since ancient astronomers first mapped them. That means someone born on 15 April with a "Western Aries Sun" typically has their sidereal Sun in Pisces (Meena). Bhagya reports the sidereal placements per classical Vedic tradition — the same convention used in every serious Sanskrit text on the subject.
