What numerology reads (and doesn't)

Numerology reads your name + birth date through a modern Western (Cheiro) or ancient Chinese (Lo Shu) framework. Vedic astrology reads your full birth chart from a completely different tradition. These are separate systems, not two views of the same underlying method — sometimes their readings coincidentally align, sometimes they don't.

Numerology cannot predict marriage timing, career changes, or specific life events — those need chart-based Jyotish analysis. What numerology can offer: a compact prompt for self-reflection on your inner drive, external personality, and how you see your own life theme. Treat it as a mirror, not an oracle.

For a genuinely Vedic reading, generate your free Janma Kundli — that's classical Jyotish from the actual Sanskrit sources this site cites.

The 5 numbers we compute (and where they come from)

  • • Life Path Number — all the digits of your birth date added together, then reduced; your reading spells out that convention and why this page uses it. Origin: L. Dow Balliett / Juno Jordan, USA, ~1908-1940s.
  • • Destiny / Expression Number — English-letter name reduction. Origin: same Balliett/Jordan lineage.
  • • Soul Urge Number — from name vowels. Origin: 20th-c. Western numerology.
  • • Personality Number — from name consonants. Origin: 20th-c. Western numerology.
  • • Name Number — the total of your name letters on the Chaldean table, then reduced; it is the same quantity as the Destiny/Expression number, under the name Indian numerology uses for it. Origin of the Chaldean table: Cheiro (William John Warner, 1866-1936); the tables in wide Indian circulation trace to him.

Conventions used in this reading

Numerology has more than one live convention for these calculations, and they give different answers. Here is what this page uses — the same way it names Chaldean for letters.

  • • Life Path — every digit of the birth date added together, then reduced (12 May 1934 → 5+1+2+1+9+3+4 = 25 → 7). This is the convention in the Vedic source material this site works from. Much modern Western numerology instead reduces the month, day and year separately and adds those three; the two methods part company for roughly one birth date in eight, always over whether the answer stays a master number or settles on its base. Where the final total lands on 11, 22 or 33, it is kept.
  • • Birthday Number — reduced all the way to 1–9. Born on the 11th is a 2, born on the 22nd is a 4; in this tradition 11 and 22 are read as shades of 2 and 4, not as numbers of their own.
  • • Personal Year — reduced all the way to 1–9. The cycle has nine steps, which is why the panel further down counts to nine.
  • • Expression, Heart Desire, Personality and Name Number — 11, 22 and 33 are kept unreduced here, because these come from the Chaldean letter-value system, in which the master numbers are part of the framework.
  • • There is no setting to switch these. One convention is applied to everyone and named here. A toggle would ask you to choose between schools you have no basis to choose between, and would leave the page unable to say what its own numbers mean.
