In short

Karva Chauth is the classical vrat (fast) observed by married women for the wellbeing and long life of their husbands. Falls on the 4th day of Krishna paksha in Kartik month (October-November). The fast is broken only after sighting the moon at night. This guide walks the 2026 timing, the vrat sequence hour-by-hour, the classical significance, and modern honest guidance on observance.

Karva Chauth 2026 date

Karva Chauth 2026: Thursday, October 29, 2026 (Kartik Krishna Chaturthi). Traditional practice includes:

  • Sargi (pre-dawn meal, offered by mother-in-law)
  • Nirjala vrat (fast without water) from sunrise
  • Evening puja at pradosh kaal with community of women
  • Moonrise darshan + arghya — the fast is broken
  • Husband feeds first bite of water and food to wife

The 2026 vrat sequence hour-by-hour

Pre-dawn (~4:30-5:00 AM local time)

Sargi meal. Traditionally prepared and offered by the mother-in-law (or an elder woman in the family). Contains: sweets (mithi mathri), a fruit (usually pomegranate), dry fruits, seviyan (vermicelli in milk), a little water. Eat slowly, hydrate well.

Sunrise to sunset (nirjala vrat)

No food, no water. Some women observe a modified vrat with water — this is regionally acceptable. Do not observe if pregnant, nursing, on medication, or with any health condition.

Late afternoon (Puja time — Pradosh Kaal)

Karva Chauth puja is performed with women dressed in wedding-red attire, holding the karva (earthen pot) and thali (plate with 16 shringar items). The Katha (story of Queen Veeravati or Karva) is recited. Roughly 5:30-7:00 PM depending on location.

Moonrise (varies by city)

The primary muhurat of the day. Once moon is sighted:

  • Look at moon through the sieve (chalni)
  • Then look at husband's face through the sieve
  • Offer arghya (water) to the moon
  • Husband feeds first sip of water and first bite of food to wife
  • Fast is broken; eat normally

Moonrise 2026 for Delhi: approximately 8:15-8:45 PM (verify with Panchang for your specific city).

Classical significance

Karva Chauth is not just a "husband's long life" ritual — classical tradition frames it as:

  • Sindoor-strengthening vrat — reinforces the marital bond ritual layer
  • Community observance — women observe together, strengthening social bonds
  • Discipline practice — annual reset of self-control and intention
  • Devotional layer — bhakti expression of care for family

Modern honest guidance

  • Health first — never do nirjala vrat if pregnant, nursing, diabetic, or on medication that requires food/water. Observe modified vrat instead.
  • Consent matters — no observance should be forced by family pressure. If a woman doesn't wish to fast, that is her right.
  • Same-sex + non-traditional families — the vrat can be observed for any beloved partner; classical tradition doesn't exclude modern family structures for those who wish to observe.
  • Some men observe fast for wife's wellbeing — modern egalitarian practice; classically not prescribed but not prohibited either

Reading Karva Chauth honestly

Karva Chauth is one of India's most emotionally-loaded vrats — significant to those who observe, meaningless to those who don't. Neither view should be imposed on the other. If you observe: do it with care for your health. If you don't: respect those who do without judgment.

Classical sources cited

Skanda Purana (Kartik Mahatmya), Bhavishya Purana (Karva Chauth Katha). Moonrise timing per Panchang tradition. Health-first modern practice per contemporary Ayurvedic guidance.
