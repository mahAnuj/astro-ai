In short

Griha Pravesh (house-warming) is the classical ceremony marking your entry into a new home. Beyond the calendar date, the ritual involves specific muhurats within the day, blessings from a priest, and specific offerings. Auspicious dates for 2026-27 run from late November 2026, when Chaturmas closes, through to the start of the next Chaturmas in mid-July 2027 — broken by two Kharmas gaps in winter and in spring. This guide walks the nine classical criteria for an auspicious Griha Pravesh, personal factors on top of the calendar, and honest guidance on modern practical constraints.

Three types of Griha Pravesh

Classical tradition names three types:

  • Apoorva — first-time entry into a brand new home built from scratch
  • Sapoorva — re-entry after long absence or after major renovation
  • Dwandwa — entry into a home you own but haven't lived in (e.g., inherited property)

Nine classical criteria for auspicious Griha Pravesh

  1. Guru (Jupiter) visible above the horizon — Jupiter must be uncombust and visible during ceremony hours
  2. Favourable moon nakshatra — Rohini, Mrigashira, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Chitra, Anuradha, Uttara Ashadha, Revati are most auspicious; avoid Bharani, Krittika, Ardra, Ashlesha
  3. Favourable tithi — 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 13 preferred; avoid 4, 8, 9, 14, 15 (Purnima/Amavasya) in most traditions
  4. No Chaturmas restriction — traditional practice avoids the Vishnu-sleeping period (mid-Jul to mid-Nov); modern flexibility varies
  5. No Kharmas — Sun in Sagittarius (Dhanurmas, roughly mid-December to mid-January) or Sun in Pisces (Meenmas, roughly mid-March to mid-April) halts all shubh karya including housewarming. Wait for the Sun to move into Capricorn or Aries respectively. The exact turning days shift each year with the sankrantis — the 2026-27 ones are listed below.
  6. No Dagdha yoga — burnt tithi-weekday pairings per Muhurta Chintamani: Sunday+Dwadashi, Monday+Ekadashi, Tuesday+Panchami, Wednesday+Tritiya, Thursday+Shashti, Friday+Ashtami, Saturday+Navami. The day is "burnt" and unfit for shubh karya.
  7. No eclipse (solar or lunar) — including the days immediately before and after
  8. No Panchak days — five-day nakshatra window (Dhanishta latter half through Revati)
  9. Weekday preference — Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday best. Tuesday and Saturday avoided (heaviness planets). Sunday depends on tradition.

Personal factors on top of the calendar

Even a universally-auspicious date should be checked against:

  • Your moon nakshatra harmony with the intended date's nakshatra
  • Your current Vimshottari Dasha (avoid Rahu-Rahu or Ketu-Ketu antardasha if possible)
  • Your current Sade Sati phase (Peak is a caution flag)
  • Any personal Rahu Kaal on the day of ceremony (must avoid that hour)

Notice that Devuthani sits inside that Panchak run and is still the strongest date of the month. That is not an oversight. Devuthani Ekadashi is an abujha (self-auspicious, "no reckoning needed") muhurat — one of the swayam-siddha days that classical tradition treats as auspicious in its own right, without a tithi-nakshatra calculation and without being cancelled by Panchak. Do not "correct" this by pushing the ceremony past Panchak: the abujha status is the override, and it is why families across North India begin the season on that day. The ordinary rules resume immediately after, which is why the next clean dates only appear once Revati has passed.

  • Makar Sankranti — Friday, 15 Jan 2027 — the Sun enters Capricorn, the winter Kharmas lifts, and the day before it is the last restricted one. From there the winter window runs through February 2027, and it is the easiest season of the year to book — no Chaturmas, no Kharmas, and the wedding rush has not yet peaked.

  • The first date the finder rates excellent falls in the third week of April 2027 — Hasta nakshatra, Shukla Trayodashi, on a Monday.

Which convention this page uses. The Sun crosses into Aries after midday on Mesha Sankranti. Our muhurat finder samples the Sun's sign at midday of each date, so it reads that whole day as still Meenmas and clears the restriction from the following day. A panchang that fixes the sankranti at the instant of ingress will call that same afternoon clear; one that fixes it by the next sunrise agrees with us. All three are defensible readings of the same event, and this page follows the midday-sampling convention throughout, because that is what the finder linked below actually applies. If your family priest reckons by the ingress instant, expect a one-day difference on exactly these turn days — and follow your priest.

Practical constraint: builder handover date

Modern life often forces a specific handover date from a developer or landlord. Classical practice for this:

  • Perform a symbolic entry (bring water, a lit diya, a small idol) on an auspicious muhurat date even if the actual moving happens on the handover date
  • Delay the full Griha Pravesh ceremony to the next auspicious window
  • Some traditions allow "provisional entry" with the full ceremony done later

Consult a priest for the specific compensating ritual that fits your situation.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the developer's handover date without astrological check — the most common modern error
  • Doing Griha Pravesh during Chaturmas without a compensating ritual
  • Missing Panchak — even if all other rules align, Panchak nullifies an ordinary date (an abujha day such as Devuthani Ekadashi is the standing exception)
  • Doing it on an eclipse-adjacent day
  • Not doing personal chart check — universally-good date can be personally difficult

Reading Griha Pravesh Muhurat honestly

The calendar rules are one layer; your specific chart is the other. Both must align. Combined with a proper Vastu check of the new home, this ceremony marks a genuinely fresh start.
