In short

Kaal Sarp Dosh (काल सर्प दोष) forms when all seven traditional planets are placed on one side of the Rahu–Ketu axis in your birth chart. The books we hold treat the pattern as a yoga, Kala Sarpa, and Rao calls it highly problematic. We hold no source for the removal pujas sold around it, and this page says where our own checker is deliberately strict.

What is Kaal Sarp Dosh?

Imagine your birth chart as a clock face. Now draw a straight line through Rahu and Ketu — they always sit exactly opposite each other, 180 degrees apart. That line splits the chart into two halves.

If all seven traditional planets (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn) fall on one side of that line — none on the other side — your chart has Kaal Sarp Dosh. The image suggested by the name: the planets are "swallowed" between the serpent's head (Rahu) and tail (Ketu).

The condition takes its name from Kaal (time/death) and Sarp (serpent). What the pattern can indicate is periods of focus or struggle in the houses Rahu and Ketu occupy, never a guaranteed misfortune. The Rahu-Ketu axis that defines the dosha is astronomically the pair of lunar nodes where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic.

What the books we hold say

We do not hold BPHS, Brihat Jataka, Saravali, Phaladeepika or Jataka Parijata, so we cannot say whether they name Kaal Sarp. The remedy industry around it is associated in popular accounts with the Trimbakeshwar temple in Maharashtra; we have not traced that to a book we hold.

The books we hold: P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's Lessons on Vedic Astrology teaches Kala Sarpa as a yoga, when all planets lie between Ketu and Rahu, calls it highly problematic, and says it usually works up to about age 40. He calls the reverse order, Kala Amrita, saintly. Shubh Bhagya counts either order as the pattern; Rao distinguishes them.

The dramatic stories around it are folk wisdom, not a claim from a book we hold. Treat any single Kaal Sarp reading, especially one urging expensive Trimbakeshwar remedies, with healthy skepticism.

The 12 named types of Kaal Sarp

Folk tradition recognises 12 specific configurations depending on which house Rahu occupies:

  • Anant — Rahu in 1st, Ketu in 7th
  • Kulik — Rahu in 2nd, Ketu in 8th
  • Vasuki — Rahu in 3rd, Ketu in 9th
  • Shankhpal — Rahu in 4th, Ketu in 10th
  • Padma — Rahu in 5th, Ketu in 11th
  • Mahapadma — Rahu in 6th, Ketu in 12th
  • Takshak — Rahu in 7th, Ketu in 1st
  • Karkotak — Rahu in 8th, Ketu in 2nd
  • Shankhachud — Rahu in 9th, Ketu in 3rd
  • Ghatak — Rahu in 10th, Ketu in 4th
  • Vishdhar — Rahu in 11th, Ketu in 5th
  • Sheshnag — Rahu in 12th, Ketu in 6th

Each type is said to emphasise different life areas. Takshak (7th house Rahu) gets flagged for marriage difficulties; Padma (5th house) for children/creativity; Mahapadma (6th) often for health and litigation. The specifics aren't worth memorising — what matters is whether the dosha is even real in your chart.

How to actually check if you have Kaal Sarp Dosh

  1. Generate a Vedic (sidereal) birth chart. Note: not Western/tropical.
  2. Find Rahu's exact degree and house.
  3. Find Ketu's exact degree (always 180° from Rahu).
  4. Check the houses of the other 7 planets.
  5. If all 7 are between Rahu and Ketu on one side: Kaal Sarp may apply.
A house-only check can mislead

A calculator that counts houses rather than degrees can flag a chart whose planets are not all on one side of the Rahu–Ketu axis (the mechanism is explained here): it treats planets in houses numerically between Rahu and Ketu as inside the pattern, ignoring the actual degree of Rahu/Ketu within their houses. If Rahu is at 28° of its house and a planet is at 5° of the next house, that planet might actually be on the opposite side of the Rahu axis, not the same side. Get the degree-precise check, not the rough house-only one. Our Kundli does this correctly.

Our position on "Partial" Kaal Sarp

Some modern (post-classical) astrologers distinguish "full" from "partial/Anshik" Kaal Sarp — the latter defined as 6 of 7 planets on one side with one straddling the axis by some small degree. We deliberately do not diagnose Partial KSD, for three reasons:

  • No definition in a book we hold. We do not hold Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, Phaladeepika or Saravali, so we cannot say whether they define a "partial" version; the pattern we check is all 7 non-shadow planets on one Rahu-Ketu semicircle, or not.
  • No agreed threshold. Different modern schools use 3°, 5° or 10°, and a looser rule flags more charts than the strict one.
  • We choose the strict rule. We would rather apply the strict rule and let a competent astrologer judge a borderline chart.

Our Kaal Sarp Dosh checker uses the strict binary rule — you either have Kaal Sarp Dosh, or you don't. If our tool says you don't have it and someone else's calculator says you have "Partial KSD," the more cautious reading is that you don't have it.

What practitioners also look at

Practice, not a held book: beyond the bare geometric pattern, popular interpreters also look at the following to judge whether Kaal Sarp produces real effects. Rao says the opposite of an override: while the pattern is present, other yogas may not function.

  • Rahu's house and dignity — Rahu in a sign of strength (Aquarius, its own sign; Virgo, its moolatrikona sign; Gemini, its exaltation sign on the reckoning we follow, though some texts say Taurus) or otherwise strong in its placement softens the dosha significantly
  • Aspects from benefics — if Jupiter (see the Jupiter in Vedic astrology guide) aspects Rahu or Ketu, the dosha's impact reduces
  • Strength of the Lagna lord — a strong rising-sign ruler protects against many doshas, Kaal Sarp included
  • Saturn-Rahu relationship — these two have a complicated dynamic; Saturn's position relative to Rahu matters. If Sade Sati is also active, the Saturn-Rahu tension often becomes noticeable, though the two transits are independent.

What practitioners describe

These are practice, not from a book we hold. Rao says only that the pattern is highly problematic and usually works to about age 40. Practitioners describe patterns such as:

  • Effort-outcome gap — work that "should" pay off doesn't, for stretches of years
  • Late-blooming success — significant achievements tend to come after age 35-40
  • Foreign or unconventional paths — Rahu pulls outward; success often comes via routes unfamiliar to family
  • Persistent low-grade obstacles — minor things going wrong frequently rather than major disasters
  • Strong spiritual inclinations — Ketu often produces deep meditative or contemplative tendencies

Softening conditions repeated in practice

No book we hold gives a Kaal Sarp cancellation list. Practice repeats that the effects diminish under conditions such as:

  • The Lagna lord is strong and well-placed
  • Jupiter is in a kendra (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th) or trine (5th, 9th)
  • Both Rahu and Ketu are in signs ruled by their traditional friends, Venus and Saturn (traditions vary on the nodes' friendships)
  • Most planets are exalted or in own sign
  • Rao says the pattern usually works up to about age 40

Remedies — what's traditionally recommended

  • Mahamrityunjaya Mantra — recite 108 times daily for 40 days; longer is better.
  • Nag Panchami observance — on the day dedicated to serpent worship (5th day of Shravan), perform Naga puja
  • Trimbakeshwar Puja — the famous Maharashtra temple is considered the most powerful site for Kaal Sarp Shanti. Many travel there specifically for the ritual.
  • Donate silver snake figurines or release them into a holy river (in regions where this is the tradition)
  • Worship Durga and Ganesha — the deities of Rahu and Ketu respectively
  • Wear a silver naga ring or chant Rahu-Ketu specific mantras
Healthy scepticism

Our view: be cautious about paying heavily for an elaborate ceremony. Before you pay, get a degree-precise check on whether the pattern applies. Simple personal practice (mantras, donations, worship of Durga and Ganesha) is inexpensive, and no book we hold says it cannot be enough. If a "remedy" asks for a lot of money quickly, take care.

Final thought

Use the pattern as a structure for understanding your life, not as a sentence to fear. Rao calls it highly problematic and also says it usually works to about age 40, so read it as a timing-bound pattern, not a life verdict.
