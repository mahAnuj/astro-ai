In short

A Vivah Muhurat (wedding muhurat) is not just a "good day" from a calendar — it's a specific window where eight classical criteria align: Guru (Jupiter) and Shukra (Venus) both visible above the horizon and uncombust, no Adhik Maas (Malamas), no Kharmas, no Chaturmas, a favourable nakshatra, a favourable tithi clear of Dagdha yoga, no Panchak, and no eclipse within a day either side. This guide walks the classical rules for 2027 wedding dates, seasonal patterns, and how personal chart-matching intersects with the calendar. What makes a Vivah Muhurat "auspicious"

Classical Vedic tradition applies eight primary rules for wedding date selection:

1. Guru + Shukra visibility (both above the horizon, not combust)

Jupiter and Venus are the two karakas of marriage (Jupiter = husband, Venus = wife). Both must be visible in the sky — not combust (within 11° of the Sun for Guru, 10° for Shukra, per the classical orbs our finder applies) and above the horizon during the day. When either is combust, marriages are traditionally postponed to a period when they emerge.

Through 2027 both karakas stay visible for the whole of the winter and spring seasons. On the orbs above, Shukra (Venus) goes combust in early July 2027 — about a week before Chaturmas opens — and does not clear until the third week of September. Traditions using a wider or narrower orb will place that first day a little earlier or later, so treat the onset as a week, not as a single fixed date.

Guru (Jupiter) is combust for about four weeks from mid-August 2027, entirely inside the Chaturmas restriction. So in 2027 the combustion blackout costs the calendar nothing that Chaturmas had not already closed.

2. No Malamas month (Adhik Maas)

The lunisolar Vedic calendar adds an extra month every ~3 years to reconcile with the solar year. This extra month is called Adhik Maas or Malamas — no auspicious ceremonies including weddings are performed inside it.

{# adhik_maas.intercalary_months(2027) == () ; (2026) -> Adhik Jyeshtha 2026-05-17..2026-06-15 ; (2029) -> Adhik Chaitra 2029-03-16..2029-04-13 — engine, run 2026-08-08 #}

There is no Adhik Maas in 2027. The intercalary month before it was Adhik Jyeshtha in 2026, and the next one is Adhik Chaitra in 2029 — so nothing in the 2027 wedding calendar is blocked by this rule, and any 2027 date list that shows a Malamas blackout has it wrong.

3. No Kharmas (Sun in Sagittarius or Pisces)

When the Sun transits Sagittarius (Dhanurmas) or Pisces (Meenmas), classical North Indian tradition halts weddings entirely. This is the "Kharmas" period — the Sun's Guru-sanchara (transit through Jupiter's own signs) is considered a time for teachership and introspection, not for householder ceremonies. This rule is often forgotten in modern date-picker tools but is a hard classical blocker.

A rashi boundary falls at an instant, not at midnight, so a page has to say which moment it reads. This page — and our Muhurat Finder — takes the Sun's sidereal rashi at midday, New Delhi. A day whose ingress lands in the afternoon therefore still counts as the old month all through. On that convention, 2027 runs:

Our own Muhurat Finder shows the same edge at Mesha Sankranti, Apr 14, 2027: it rejects that day for Meenmas, drops the Kharmas block from the 15th, and rates the 16th the first excellent wedding day of the spring season. Traditions that reckon from the ingress instant itself, rather than from a fixed sampling time, treat the hours after the transit as already clear — a convention difference, not an error on either side.

4. Chaturmas restriction (mid-Jul to mid-Nov)

The four months from Devshayani Ekadashi to Devuthani Ekadashi (Vishnu's sleeping period) are traditionally weddings-restricted. Modern practice varies — many regional traditions permit weddings during Chaturmas with specific compensating rituals, but classical tradition prescribes waiting until Vishnu wakes in mid-November.

The 2027 window is 119 days:

  • Devuthani Ekadashi — Wednesday, Nov 10, 2027 — Chaturmas closes.

5. Favourable nakshatras

Classical wedding-auspicious nakshatras: Rohini, Mrigashira, Magha, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Swati, Anuradha, Mula, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati. Nakshatras to avoid: Bharani, Krittika, Ardra, Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Vishakha, Purva Phalguni, Purva Ashadha, Purva Bhadrapada, Chitra (partial). Check the specific nakshatra of the intended date via Panchang.

6. Favourable tithis + no Dagdha yoga

Auspicious tithis (lunar days) for weddings: 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13 (both bright and dark halves). Avoid: 4 (Chaturthi), 8 (Ashtami), 9 (Navami), 14 (Chaturdashi), 15 (Purnima/Amavasya) except in specific regional variants.

Dagdha ("burnt") tithi-weekday combinations — per Muhurta Chintamani, certain tithi-weekday pairings are "burnt" and unfit for shubh karya even if all other rules align: Sunday+Dwadashi (12), Monday+Ekadashi (11), Tuesday+Panchami (5), Wednesday+Tritiya (3), Thursday+Shashti (6), Friday+Ashtami (8), Saturday+Navami (9). Check for these before committing to a date.

7. No Panchak days

Panchak — the five-day period when the Moon transits the last five nakshatras (Dhanishta latter half, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati). Weddings are traditionally avoided during Panchak.

8. No eclipse days (±1 day)

Solar and lunar eclipses are absolute wedding blockers. The classical restriction extends to the day before and the day after — the atmosphere is considered energetically unsettled even after grahana sutak formally ends.

Personal chart considerations beyond the calendar

A universally-auspicious date can still be inauspicious FOR A SPECIFIC PERSON if:

  • The date's nakshatra clashes with the bride's or groom's birth nakshatra (Nadi mismatch, etc.)
  • The date falls during either person's Sade Sati Peak phase (some traditions recommend avoiding)
  • Either person is in a Rahu or Ketu Antardasha (soft caution)
  • Manglik Dosh matching hasn't been done for the couple

Common mistakes when choosing a Vivah Muhurat

  • Choosing a date only by tithi — nakshatra + Guru+Shukra + Panchak status matter equally
  • Ignoring Chaturmas to fit venue availability — modern practice varies but traditional weddings during Chaturmas are said to lack full blessing
  • Not doing individual chart check — the "universally good" date may be personally difficult
  • Trusting online random-date calculators that ignore combustion, Panchak, or personal chart factors
  • Choosing an eclipse day or the day immediately after — huge inauspicious classical marker
