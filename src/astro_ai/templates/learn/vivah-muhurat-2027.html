In short

A Vivah Muhurat (wedding muhurat) is not just a "good day" from a calendar — it's a specific window where six classical criteria align: Guru (Jupiter) and Shukra (Venus) both visible above the horizon (not combust), no Malamas month, no Chaturmas, favourable nakshatra + tithi, no Panchak, and no Rahu Kaal within the ceremony hour. This guide walks the classical rules for 2027 wedding dates, seasonal patterns, and how personal chart-matching intersects with the calendar. What makes a Vivah Muhurat "auspicious"

Classical Vedic tradition applies eight primary rules for wedding date selection:

1. Guru + Shukra visibility (both above the horizon, not combust)

Jupiter and Venus are the two karakas of marriage (Jupiter = husband, Venus = wife). Both must be visible in the sky — not combust (within ~11° of the Sun) and above the horizon during the day. When either is combust, marriages are traditionally postponed to a period when they emerge.

For 2027, key Guru + Shukra visibility windows: mid-Jan to Feb, mid-Apr to mid-Jul, and mid-Nov onwards. Late-summer combustion of both occurs and blackout follows.

2. No Malamas month (Adhik Maas)

The lunisolar Vedic calendar adds an extra month every ~3 years to reconcile with the solar year. This extra month is called Adhik Maas or Malamas — no auspicious ceremonies including weddings. Verify with Drik Panchang for 2027 Adhik Maas dates.

3. No Kharmas (Sun in Sagittarius or Pisces)

When the Sun transits Sagittarius (Dhanurmas, roughly mid-December to mid-January) or Pisces (Meenmas, roughly mid-March to mid-April), classical North Indian tradition halts weddings entirely. This is the "Kharmas" period — the Sun's Guru-sanchara (transit through Jupiter's own signs) is considered a time for teachership and introspection, not for householder ceremonies. Weddings resume the day after Makar Sankranti (~Jan 14) and the day after the Sun exits Pisces (~Apr 14). This rule is often forgotten in modern date-picker tools but is a hard classical blocker.

4. Chaturmas restriction (mid-Jul to mid-Nov)

The four months from Dev Shayani Ekadashi to Dev Uthani Ekadashi (Vishnu's sleeping period) are traditionally weddings-restricted. Modern practice varies — many regional traditions permit weddings during Chaturmas with specific compensating rituals, but classical tradition prescribes waiting until Dev Uthani (mid-November).

5. Favourable nakshatras

Classical wedding-auspicious nakshatras: Rohini, Mrigashira, Magha, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Swati, Anuradha, Mula, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati. Nakshatras to avoid: Bharani, Krittika, Ardra, Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Vishakha, Purva Phalguni, Purva Ashadha, Purva Bhadrapada, Chitra (partial). Check the specific nakshatra of the intended date via Panchang.

6. Favourable tithis + no Dagdha yoga

Auspicious tithis (lunar days) for weddings: 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13 (both bright and dark halves). Avoid: 4 (Chaturthi), 8 (Ashtami), 9 (Navami), 14 (Chaturdashi), 15 (Purnima/Amavasya) except in specific regional variants.

Dagdha ("burnt") tithi-weekday combinations — per Muhurta Chintamani, certain tithi-weekday pairings are "burnt" and unfit for shubh karya even if all other rules align: Sunday+Dwadashi (12), Monday+Ekadashi (11), Tuesday+Panchami (5), Wednesday+Tritiya (3), Thursday+Shashti (6), Friday+Ashtami (8), Saturday+Navami (9). Check for these before committing to a date.

7. No Panchak days

Panchak — the five-day period when the Moon transits the last five nakshatras (Dhanishta latter half, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati). Weddings are traditionally avoided during Panchak.

8. No eclipse days (±1 day)

Solar and lunar eclipses are absolute wedding blockers. The classical restriction extends to the day before and the day after — the atmosphere is considered energetically unsettled even after grahana sutak formally ends.

Abujha Muhurats — universally auspicious days that override calculation

Certain "swayam-siddha" (self-perfected) days are traditionally treated as universally auspicious for weddings regardless of tithi/nakshatra/weekday — no muhurat calculation needed. For 2027:

  • Basant Panchami — Jan 23, 2027 (Magha Shukla Panchami, Saraswati Puja day)
  • Akshaya Tritiya — May 19, 2027 (Vaishakha Shukla Tritiya, "eternal" day)
  • Bhadli Navami — Jun 13, 2027 (Ashadha Shukla Navami; regionally significant in Rajasthan/MP)
  • Devuthani Ekadashi — Oct 31, 2027 (Kartik Shukla Ekadashi, Vishnu awakens from Chaturmas)
  • Tulsi Vivah — Nov 1, 2027 (ceremonial marriage of Tulsi to Shaligram; extremely auspicious)

Seasonal wedding patterns in 2027

Given the rules above, 2027 wedding dates cluster in specific windows:

  • Mid-January to February 2027 — winter window after Makar Sankranti (~Jan 14) ends Kharmas. First auspicious dates typically around Jan 19-20 (after Kharmas + Panchak clear).
  • Mid-April to June 2027 — spring/summer window after Meenmas ends (~Apr 14). Most popular season in North India.
  • Late-October to December 2027 — post-Chaturmas resumption from Devuthani (Oct 31) through mid-December before next Kharmas begins.

Blackout windows: Adhik Maas (verify dates), Kharmas (Jan 1-14 and Mar 14-Apr 14, 2027), July-October Chaturmas, and dates immediately around solar/lunar eclipses.

Personal chart considerations beyond the calendar

A universally-auspicious date can still be inauspicious FOR A SPECIFIC PERSON if:

  • The date's nakshatra clashes with the bride's or groom's birth nakshatra (Nadi mismatch, etc.)
  • The date falls during either person's Sade Sati Peak phase (some traditions recommend avoiding)
  • Either person is in a Rahu or Ketu Antardasha (soft caution)
  • Manglik Dosh matching hasn't been done for the couple

Common mistakes when choosing a Vivah Muhurat

  • Choosing a date only by tithi — nakshatra + Guru+Shukra + Panchak status matter equally
  • Ignoring Chaturmas to fit venue availability — modern practice varies but traditional weddings during Chaturmas are said to lack full blessing
  • Not doing individual chart check — the "universally good" date may be personally difficult
  • Trusting online random-date calculators that ignore combustion, Panchak, or personal chart factors
  • Choosing an eclipse day or the day immediately after — huge inauspicious classical marker
