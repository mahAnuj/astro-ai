In short

Hasta is the thirteenth nakshatra — Virgo 10°00' to 23°20'. Its archetype is the skilled hand — craft, dexterity, healing touch, the ability to do what other hands can't. Ruled by the Moon with Savitar (the Sun as inspirer) as its deity, symbolised by an open palm, Hasta natives carry the classical qualities of manual skill combined with a mothering, healing quality.

Hasta at a glance

  • Sanskrit name: Hastā (हस्ता) — "the hand"
  • Zodiac range: Virgo 10°00' to 23°20' (sidereal)
  • Ruling planet: Moon (Chandra)
  • Deity: Savitar — the Sun in his role as inspirer and awakener; the deity of the Gayatri Mantra
  • Symbol: Open palm; hand with fingers extended
  • Gana: Deva (godly temperament)
  • Guna: Rajas (active, driven)
  • Yoni: Buffalo (paired with Swati)
  • Nadi: Adi (Vata / air dosha)
  • Nature (activity): Kshipra (swift/light) — favourable for learning, transactions, trade, medicine

The hand as instrument of Savitar's inspiration

Savitar is one of the oldest and most sacred deities in the Vedas — the aspect of the Sun that inspires, that awakens human awareness. The Gayatri Mantra, one of the most powerful classical prayers, is addressed to Savitar. What Savitar inspires, the human being executes with their hands.

This is the Hasta connection. The nakshatra takes the divine inspiration (Savitar's spark) and delivers it through the physical hand. Whether the hand is a healer's, a craftsman's, an artist's, a surgeon's, a magician's, or a mother's stroking a child's forehead — the skill that flows through the hand is Savitar's blessing made manifest.

The five gifts of the hand

Classical texts sometimes describe Hasta's five gifts, one per finger: creation (thumb — bringing things into being), direction (index — pointing the way), power (middle — the longest, the reach), expression (ring — beauty and adornment), and subtlety (little finger — fine detail work). Hasta natives usually have visible dexterity — you notice their hands moving as they speak.

The Hasta archetype: skilled, nurturing, quietly capable

Hasta natives — born with Moon between 10°00' and 23°20' of Virgo — tend to:

  • Have visibly skilled hands — good handwriting, deft cooking, capable at any manual craft they attempt
  • Attract healing and helping situations — the person others call when they need physical care, first aid, or a steady hand under pressure
  • Combine Virgo's precision with Moon's care — meticulous but warm, detailed but not cold
  • Be excellent at anything requiring both technique and empathy — massage, physiotherapy, cooking, teaching young children
  • Have a talent for the "small right gesture" — the perfectly-timed touch on someone's shoulder, the note left at the exact moment needed
  • Attract abundance through service — Hasta is one of the classical nakshatras for accumulating wealth through skilled work

The shadow side: perfectionism. The skill-orientation can tip into never-being-satisfied-with-the-work. Combined with Virgo's already-critical mind, Hasta natives can drive themselves (and collaborators) hard trying to reach a standard that keeps moving. The Moon rulership softens this — the mature Hasta learns that "good enough, delivered with warmth" often serves people better than "perfect, delivered late."

Career: where Hasta thrives

  • Medicine — especially surgery, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, dentistry, obstetrics — the classical "healer's hand"
  • Massage, bodywork, Ayurvedic marma therapy — literal Hasta portfolio
  • Craft trades — carpentry, jewellery-making, tailoring, watchmaking, luthiery
  • Culinary arts — chefs, pastry, food styling
  • Fine arts — painting, calligraphy, sculpture, ceramics
  • Teaching young children — the Moon's nurturing quality applied
  • Astrology, palmistry, tarot — Hasta is the classical nakshatra of hand-reading
  • Magic and illusion — sleight-of-hand is literal Hasta work

Where Hasta natives struggle: purely abstract intellectual roles with no physical outlet; environments where their care for detail is dismissed as slowness; work that requires them to skip the meticulous step.

Relationships and matching

Hasta is Deva gana (refined) with Adi nadi (Vata/air).

  • Best matches: Deva-gana partners with Madhya or Antya nadi — Pushya (Madhya, often cited as a classical strong Hasta partner), Anuradha (Madhya), Revati (Antya), Shravana (Antya). Same-nadi partners (any other Adi-nakshatra: Ashwini, Ardra, Punarvasu, Uttara Phalguni, Jyeshtha, Mula, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada) lose 8 Nadi Kuta points.
  • Challenging matches: Same-Adi-nadi partners (above list) lose 8 Nadi Kuta points. Rakshasa-gana partners create Gana-Kuta friction.

Hasta natives in partnership bring care, service, and reliable competence. They often express love through practical acts (cooking, tending, fixing what's broken) rather than through grand declarations. The classical happy Hasta marriage involves a partner who receives care gracefully — Hasta doesn't need constant reciprocation but does need to feel that their service is seen.

Health tendencies

Hasta's Nadi is Adi (Vata / air), and Virgo governs the intestines and lower digestive tract in the Kalapurusha.

  • Digestive emphasis — Virgo body region; Hasta natives are prone to IBS, food sensitivities, and gut-related issues (Vata-nadi typically manifests as anxiety-driven gut symptoms rather than sluggish digestion)
  • Nervous-system emphasis — Vata constitution: tendency to insomnia, restlessness, dry skin, and joint dryness with age
  • Hand/wrist injuries common — the tool of the trade takes the wear; carpal tunnel, RSI, arthritis of hands with age
  • Overwork burnout — Hasta's perfectionism plus willingness-to-help combines badly if unchecked

Classical support: warming spices for Kapha digestion (ginger, black pepper, turmeric), regular hand-and-wrist self-massage with warm oil, boundaries around work hours, cooked-warm foods over cold-raw.

Vimshottari Dasha implications

Hasta's ruler is the Moon — so Hasta natives begin life in a Moon Mahadasha (10 years). Same signature as Rohini: early childhood shaped by the mother, emotional formation, home life. Then the 7-year Mars Mahadasha kicks in around age 8-10 — often experienced as "childhood ending suddenly", when the world's demands become more active.

Hasta natives typically enter their Rahu Mahadasha (18 years) around late teens — often the "start of the real career" phase, and where the Hasta manual-skill combines with Rahu's ambition to launch their professional identity.

Muhurta suitability

Hasta is Kshipra (swift/light) — favourable for many kinds of skilled work.

  • Starting medical treatment, first day of therapy, starting rehabilitation
  • Beginning a craft apprenticeship or learning a manual skill
  • Signing trade contracts, opening a shop, launching a product
  • Fine detail work — surgery scheduling (elective), watchmaking projects, restoration projects
  • Astrology consultations, palmistry readings — Hasta's own domain
  • Weddings — Hasta is one of the acceptable wedding nakshatras (Deva-gana Kshipra combination)

Avoid: heavy confrontational actions, spiritual retreats requiring detachment from doing.
