{# Form-first on mobile (#195). THE FORM COLUMN COMES FIRST IN THE SOURCE, just above this block — that ordering is load-bearing, not incidental. Below `md` there is one column, so DOM order IS the visual order and the form leads. `md:order-1` here and `md:order-2` on the form restore prose-left / form-right at `md` and up, where `md:grid-cols-2` makes two columns. Verified at 1280: h1 at x=88 y=333, form at x=672 y=277 — identical to the pre-#195 layout. This reorder is the only lever that reached the 667px fold. The two commits before it (spacing/typography reclaim, then hiding the form sub-line) took the CTA bottom on 375×667 from 1119 to 996 to 956 and stalled there, still 289px short. Form-first lands it at 623 — 44px above the fold. Measure with scripts/measure_cta_fold.py, not by eye. WHY THE SOURCE ORDER, AND NOT CSS `order` ALONE The first version of this did it purely with `order-1`/`order-2` and left the DOM as prose-then-form. That looked identical and was wrong: tab order follows the DOM, not `order`. On mobile the two provenance-chip links were focused BEFORE the form fields while rendering BELOW them, so a keyboard user tabbed past the form, into content beneath it, then back up into the form — one backward transition of -532px, WCAG 2.4.3 (Focus Order). Moving the nodes instead of only their painted position makes visual, DOM and focus order agree below `md`: the same sweep now reports zero backward transitions there. (An earlier version of this note and of the commit message said "five backward jumps". That was a counting artefact — the sweep compared each stop against a running maximum rather than against the previous stop, so one real reversal was reported five times. Independent QA measured one. The defect was real; the number was not.) If you reorder anything in this hero again, re-run the keyboard sweep, at BOTH breakpoints. A pure `order-*` change cannot be validated by looking at it, and neither can this one. THE TWO ACCEPTED COSTS 1. DESKTOP FOCUS ORDER. One DOM order has to serve two visual layouts, so whichever breakpoint does not match the source eats the mismatch. It is now desktop's turn: from `md` up the form is painted right but comes first in source, so a keyboard user tabs the whole right-hand form before either left-column link, then jumps back up 313px. `main` was not clean here either (a 53px step across the column boundary), but its order did not reverse the columns and this one does. This is the deliberate trade — mobile is the primary platform — not an oversight. Shrinking the prose enough to avoid the reorder entirely was measured and falls ~289px short. 2. HEADING ORDER.
