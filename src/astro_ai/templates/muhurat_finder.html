Combustion (asta): this finder calls a planet combust when, at noon on the date, it is within a fixed number of degrees of the Sun: Guru 11°, Shukra 10° (9° when retrograde), Budh 14° (13° when retrograde). Those orbs are from B.V. Raman's Muhurtha. Applying Guru and Shukra combustion as a hard "avoid" for Vivah is this finder's own convention: Raman's wedding criteria and Rao's Table 79 wedding guidelines do not list combustion (Raman applies Guru and Shukra combustion to other ceremonies, such as Chowlam, but not to marriage). A combust planet marks the date "avoid" outright, but only for the events that check it: Guru for Vivah, Griha Pravesh and Vidyarambh; Shukra for Vivah; Budh for Business. Namkaran checks none of them. The finder does not count days around a planet's rising or setting in the sky, and it knows only "combust" or "not combust". For Business, Budh combustion blocks every weekday, not only Wednesday; that is this finder's own extension, not a rule we source.

Chandra Bala: counted from the natal Moon. A house is shown as weak when either of two works by Raman names it: Muhurtha or Electional Astrology (for an election chart, the Moon in the 6th, 8th or 12th from the Janma Rasi) or his Gochara table for the Moon in Astrology for Beginners (houses 2, 4, 5, 8, 9 and 12). Tara Bala, Janma: on a day when the Moon is in the birth star of the person being matched (Tara position 1, Janma), the personal match still shows "ok", the same for every event; it is not varied by event. The Vivah couple verdict treats the two partners differently, following B.V. Raman's Muhurtha or Electional Astrology: a groom on the day ruled by his own birth star is no longer counted as a pass, because Raman advises avoiding the day ruled by the 1st star and lists marriage among the events it is inauspicious for; a bride on her own birth-star day still passes, because he says that day is quite favourable for a woman's marriage. The count is mod 9, so the 10th and 19th stars from the birth star fall here too. This finder reads the whole-day nakshatra, so it does not apply the exception Raman allows only in urgent cases, where just the opening ghatis of that star are avoided. The same passage is unclear on "nuptials", so that sentence is not used. The nine Tara names shown are Raman’s own, from the same chapter, and he lists the days ruled by the 1st, 3rd, 5th and 7th stars as ones to avoid. The finder marks the 3rd, 5th and 7th (Vipat, Pratyak, Naidhana) avoid, and treats the 1st (Janma) as described above. Splitting the other five into good and excellent is this site’s own grading, not sourced to a text this site holds.
