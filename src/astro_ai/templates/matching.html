What is Ashtakoot Guna Milan?

Ashtakoot Guna Milan ("eight-fold merit matching") is the classical Vedic system for scoring the compatibility of two natal charts for marriage. Total possible: 36 points, distributed across eight kootas (compatibility axes). A score of around 18/36 is the commonly used "acceptable" floor; 24+/36 is widely read as strong; 30+/36 is rare and excellent. Those cut-offs are a rule of thumb — Bhagya's own reading of the total, not drawn from the classical texts we hold. But the raw score alone is only half the picture — Bhakoot dosha, Nadi dosha, and Manglik status can override or be cancelled by other placements, and a good astrologer weighs the felt-life implications of each koota separately rather than just checking the total.

The 8 kootas

Which chart goes in which side matters. Two of the eight kootas — Varna and Gana — are scored directionally in the classical tradition: the same two charts can score differently depending on which partner is the groom and which is the bride. Put the groom's details in the groom's section and the bride's in the bride's. This is not a formality — swapping them changes the result, because the tradition gives the two directions different verdicts.

  • • Varna (1 point) — spiritual temperament comparison based on Moon-sign class (Brahmin/Kshatriya/Vaishya/Shudra). Bride's Varna should not exceed groom's.
  • • Vashya (2 points) — mutual attraction based on Moon-sign category (human/quadruped/aquatic/etc). Certain pairings hold natural sway over others.
  • • Tara (3 points) — nakshatra-star compatibility. Bhagya's tool credits position 8 (Ati-Mitra) as favourable per BPHS — most free tools mark it neutral, dropping a real classical positive.
  • • Yoni (4 points) — animal-symbol compatibility of the birth nakshatras. 14 yoni types, with defined friend/enemy pairings.
  • • Graha Maitri (5 points) — friendship between the Moon-lords of the two charts. The single most emotionally important koota.
  • • Gana (6 points) — Deva / Manushya / Rakshasa temperament. Ideally match; Deva-Rakshasa is the most difficult.
  • • Bhakoot (7 points) — 12-house Moon-sign distance. Certain distances (6-8, 5-9, 2-12) traditionally score zero. Cancellations Bhagya applies: same rashi-lord for both partners, or Naisargika (natural) friendship between the two Moon-lords.
  • • Nadi (8 points) — the highest-weight koota, ostensibly about progeny and hereditary compatibility. Same-Nadi pairings score zero. Cancellations Bhagya applies: same nakshatra but different padas, or same rashi with different nakshatras.

Manglik matching — beyond the checkbox

Manglik (Mangal) Dosh is the classical concern about Mars-placement causing marital friction. Bhagya's matching goes beyond "Mars in 1st/4th/7th/8th/12th house from Lagna". We also check Mars from the Moon and Mars from Venus (the three-axis rule), and apply the cancellations it implements — both partners Manglik at comparable severity; Mars in own or exalted sign; Jupiter aspecting Mars or the 7th house; and a few others. Read the full Manglik Dosh explainer for the classical logic.

Beyond Ashtakoot — what matching does NOT check

A good astrologer doesn't stop at Guna Milan. A fuller reading also considers:

  • • 7th house of each chart (marriage house), its lord, and any planets there.
  • • Venus (Shukra) in both charts — natural karaka of love and marriage.
  • • Dashas active around any proposed marriage date — Venus/Jupiter Dashas favour marriage; Rahu/Ketu can bring unusual partnerships.
  • • Sade Sati and Ashtama Shani transits — Saturn's 7.5-year phases can strain any relationship.
  • • Bhava Bala (house strength) via Ashtakvarga — quantifies which houses can carry their significations.

This is why we cross-link to Manglik Check and Sade Sati Check from the results page. Ashtakoot is the fast screening tool; a full compatibility review requires all these layers.
