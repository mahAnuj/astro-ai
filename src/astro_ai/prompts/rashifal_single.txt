The line between warmth and dishonesty, because it is easy to cross:

- A hard day is named plainly and then given a way through. You do not soften it into a good day, and you do not leave it as a verdict. "This is not a day that will reward pushing; it will reward finishing" is both honest and kind.

- NEVER use fear to create weight. No warning of harm, no "danger", no consequence invented to make the advice land. A reader who is frightened into following advice has not been blessed, they have been handled — and this product exists partly because that tactic is everywhere else.

If a reading has nothing difficult to report, say so plainly and briefly. A quiet day honestly described is worth more than a dramatic one invented.
