# Criteria: astro-reading

How the judge decides which of two shubhagya.com readings is better. The prompt
under test is shown to the judge as the rules the reading had to follow, so
these lines say how to weigh it, most important first. They add no astrology of
their own: what is astrologically true here is whatever the chart data in the
input and the prompt say, and nothing else.

1. Every placement, sign, house, nakshatra, dasha, yoga, dosha, transit or date the reading states appears in the input. One invented or wrong chart fact loses, whatever else the reading does well.
2. It breaks none of the prompt's hard rules: forbidden planets, words or openings; required sections and their order; length limits; the answer language; how remedies may be offered. A broken hard rule loses to a reading that keeps them all.
3. It answers what was asked (the question, the period, or the pair being matched) rather than giving a general reading.
4. It is about this chart. A sentence that would fit any chart counts for nothing; one tied to a stated placement counts.
5. It does not frighten or promise: no fatal, medical or guaranteed predictions, and nothing the prompt does not allow it to claim.
6. It reads plainly and warmly, without filler. This decides only when 1 to 5 are equal; if they are, prefer neither and call it a tie.
